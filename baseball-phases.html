BASEBALL PHASES 1–3

Pitcher-focused pre-throwing movement, power preparation, and post-throwing recovery.

PHASE 1 · PRE-THROWING WARM-UP

Breath (Taio) — Hip-Shoulder Separator

Stand in an athletic stance with a light band around the lower ribs. Breathe into 360° rib expansion, then exhale while keeping the neck and shoulders relaxed. The goal is controlled rib-cage motion without unnecessary upper-body tension.

Board (Hoko) — Back-Leg Foundation

Balance on your back pitching leg on a stable low block, pad, or floor and lift the lead leg. Maintain big-toe, little-toe, and heel contact without letting the knee collapse inward. Goal: 60 seconds.

Bar (Kankotsu) — Rotational Extension

Hold an alignment rod across the shoulders and step into a controlled stride. Keep the pelvis stable while rotating the upper trunk over the lead leg through a comfortable range.

Bowl (Tandem) — Hinge & Hip Load

With a wide stance, locate your center of mass and shift into the hips while keeping the trunk organized. Practice loading the back hip without collapsing through the spine.

Bridge (Sekizui) — Thoracic Motion

On hands and knees, perform smooth spinal waves with special attention to comfortable thoracic extension. Do not force end range; thoracic mobility is only one part of managing throwing stress.

PHASE 2 · POWER & MOMENTUM

Judo Rolls (Ukemi) — Deceleration & Body Control

Practice controlled rolls only on an appropriate surface and with sound technique. Use them as general spatial-awareness and momentum drills rather than as a substitute for baseball-specific deceleration training.

Handstand Variations — Scapular Control

Use a wall handstand, wall walk, or inverted bear position. Push the floor away and maintain active shoulder-blade upward rotation with rib control. Goal: 30–45 seconds with no shoulder pain.

Featherweight Javelin Throwing — Sequencing

Use a light training javelin/foam implement in a clear area. Let the pelvis and trunk initiate while the arm follows smoothly. Goal: 10–15 relaxed throws. A clean flight can be useful feedback, but wobble alone does not diagnose an elbow- or shoulder-dominant throw. Stop with pain.

PHASE 3 · ARM RECOVERY

Post-throw breathing can help you downshift, but it does not “flush lactic acid” or instantly accelerate muscle recovery. Use it as a relaxation and heart-rate recovery practice.

Post-Throwing Downshift

High-arousal / hot-day option: breathe comfortably around 4 seconds in and 6–8 seconds out for several minutes, without straining or breath-holding.

Cool-day / neutral option: use relaxed, even breathing around 4 seconds in and 4 seconds out.

Finish lying comfortably for up to 5 minutes, scanning for unnecessary tension. Moon phase can be used as a personal ritual cue, but it does not need to determine the breathing protocol.
